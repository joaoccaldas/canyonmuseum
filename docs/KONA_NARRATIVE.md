# KONA Narrative Spine V1

Status: canonical product-story contract.  
Scope: public product voice, editorial framing and fictionalized builder context.  
Non-goal: biography.

## One sentence

**KONA is a place for triathlon, curiosity and whatever comes next, built in public by someone following one interesting thing into another and seeing where it leads.**

The athlete is the main character of the product. The builder is background context.

## The Intern

KONA may occasionally refer to an anonymous remote intern.

The intern is a narrative device, not a mascot, founder profile or navigation concept.

The fiction is deliberately small:

- he was hired online to try building something for triathletes;
- he has a past connection to triathlon and likes numbers;
- he left a comfortable, predictable career and is trying something different;
- he is learning parts of the job while doing them;
- he does not yet pretend to know exactly what KONA will become.

KONA never confirms a real identity. Do not publish a real name, employer history, age, family information, salary, home address, identifying photograph or private biography through the intern story.

## The emotional center

The deeper story is not “an intern built an app.”

It is using making, sport, curiosity and technology as a way to communicate when ordinary explanation is not enough.

The product should quietly leave room for:

- starting again;
- learning in public;
- following curiosity without knowing the complete destination;
- making something as a way of understanding it;
- discovering new people, subjects and possibilities;
- being serious about the work without pretending to have every answer.

Do not explain this on every screen. Users should be able to feel it before they can summarize it.

## The writing constitution

**KONA always knows WHAT it needs to say. It is allowed to experiment with HOW it says it.**

### WHAT is stable

Facts, sources, navigation, actions, privacy, safety, product state and important instructions must be:

- accurate;
- understandable;
- source-grounded where appropriate;
- honest about uncertainty;
- consistent in meaning.

### HOW may vary

Expression can sometimes be:

- extremely concise;
- unexpectedly verbose;
- editorial;
- technical;
- handwritten;
- serious;
- dryly funny;
- slightly absurd;
- quietly vulnerable.

Variation is a feature only when the meaning stays intact.

## Hard rule

**Never sacrifice usability to demonstrate personality.**

A funny error still explains the problem and recovery action.

A strange loading message still communicates that something is loading.

A Kona Now summary still tells the user what happened before adding personality.

A hidden room can be mysterious. The exit cannot be.

## Personality budget

Default to approximately:

- 90% product doing its job;
- 10% visible intern fingerprints.

The intern becomes less interesting if every screen mentions him.

Use him for moments, not chrome.

Good surfaces:
- Kona Now editorial notes;
- About KONA;
- release notes;
- FieldNote artifacts;
- rare loading/error recovery copy;
- hidden 3D objects or rooms;
- experimental features that benefit from transparent context.

Avoid:
- primary navigation labels;
- safety/privacy copy;
- account deletion;
- transactional confirmations;
- factual source attribution;
- every card or button.

## Editorial behavior

Kona Now is the clearest public expression of the intern voice.

Order of operations:

1. State the useful fact.
2. Explain why it is worth the user's attention.
3. Add personality only if it improves the read.
4. Keep the original source one obvious action away.
5. Never imply first-hand knowledge the source does not provide.

Example shape:

> **The useful thing**
>
> Two or three sentences explaining what happened and why it matters.
>
> Optional intern line.
>
> **Read / watch the original →**

The personality layer must never change the factual claim.

## About KONA

About KONA should offer three depths without forcing any of them:

### Short version

A small curiosity became another small curiosity, then a bike, then a room, then a world. Welcome to KONA.

### The scenic route

Explain that KONA grew by following questions across sport, machines, places, people, software and storytelling. The point is exploration, not a perfectly planned origin story.

### The unnecessarily complete version

Let the form itself become part of the story: detours, diagrams, old experiments, failed ideas and things learned along the way.

The three versions must tell the same underlying truth. They vary in HOW, not WHAT.

## Feedback and vulnerability

The intern can admit that KONA is evolving.

That must never become an excuse for broken software.

Preferred posture:

- “This is an experiment. Tell us what is confusing.”
- “We changed this because the old version made no sense on a phone.”
- “I am still figuring out the best way to explain this.”

Avoid:

- joking away data loss or security;
- blaming the intern for defects;
- asking users to tolerate preventable failures;
- fake self-deprecation;
- startup mythology.

## Product architecture mapping

The story follows the existing information architecture. It does not create another one.

- **Home**: the user's current page. Intern presence is very light.
- **Discover**: curiosity made navigable.
- **Garage**: machines, evidence and obsessive detail.
- **Plan**: responsible, practical race-week information.
- **Me / User Studio**: the user's identity and progress. The intern stays out of the way.
- **Kona Now**: strongest editorial voice.
- **3D world**: deeper narrative artifacts can be discovered rather than announced.

No “Intern” top-level tab.

## Future depth

Later product work may add an unlockable Intern's Desk or archive of abandoned experiments.

It should contain development archaeology, not personal biography: rejected UI, early models, notes, prototypes, lessons and clues about future work.

This is intentionally not launch-critical.

## Acceptance test for new copy

Before shipping expressive KONA copy, ask:

1. Can a new user still understand the literal meaning immediately?
2. Is the factual part true and sourced where necessary?
3. Would the sentence work if the intern joke were removed?
4. Is this an appropriate place for personality?
5. Are we revealing anything about a real person that the product does not need?
6. Does this feel authored rather than “quirky brand copy”?

If any answer is no, simplify it.
